Chapter One

A portrait of Avneesha.

Because I don't think you see yourself the way the rest of us see you. So here, let me try.

a pure soul with more ambition than she gives herself credit for, and a child inside her that she never let the world take away

a lawyer by profession, but a Bharatnatyam dancer at heart — argument and art, living in the same person

always the one giving people another chance to stay, another reason to hold on, another hand to pull the bond back together

strong enough to stand alone, humble enough to set her ego aside for the people she loves, hard on the outside, impossibly soft inside

And always — always — doing more than enough for everyone she cares about, and everyone she finds in need.

Chapter Three

Our greatest hits.

Small, specific, ordinary moments I'll carry for the rest of my life.

Memory One

A parked car at Marine Drive. Your head on my shoulder. Your hand in mine. I remember that evening more clearly than most days of my life.

Memory Two

You, holding my hand while I drove. A manual car. I didn't change gears the entire ride. I'd rather have stalled on the road than let go of your hand.

Memory Three

Pune. You holding my hand as you fell asleep. You said the touch made you feel safe. I never told you, it made me feel like I had a purpose.

Memory Four

Those video calls that lasted for hours. One of us falling asleep, the other quietly working with the call still on. Somehow, that was its own kind of togetherness.

before we continue

you crossed countries. you finished what you started. you came back softer.

sometimes i just want to pause and remind you of what you've already done — before you forget again.

Chapter Four

A letter, because you need to hear this.

My dearest Anvi,

I've been trying to write this for days. Every version started sounding like something printed inside a greeting card, and you know that's not how I talk to you. So I'm writing this the way I would if you were sitting next to me and quietly stealing my coffee.

This year has been heavy for you. I've heard it in the pauses between your sentences and in the way your voice changes when I ask how you actually are. You carry a lot without saying much about it. You always have. And I just want you to know, I see it. I've always seen it.

You've been handling too many things at once: the job search, unexplained rejections (which are honestly very rude), part-time work, a sister's wedding, health, family, expectations, all of it. And somehow, even inside all that, you still show up for everyone else. You still check on me. That's not ordinary strength. That's just very you.

There's something I don't think I've ever said properly: when I moved to the US and everything felt unfamiliar, I held it together because I had already watched you do it first. Quietly. Without making a big thing of how hard it was. I remember thinking, if she can do this, I can too. You made that year easier for me just by being who you are.

You forget this about yourself, so I'm writing it here: you've already survived everything that was supposed to break you. The past. The health scares. You crossed countries on your own, finished your master's, and somehow came back softer instead of harder. Most people build walls. You didn't.

About the job and all the waiting right now, you don't have to prove anything. Not to your family. Not to your friends. Not even to that version of yourself that keeps moving the finish line further away. You were already enough before any of this. You still are.

Also, the world has a very consistent habit of eventually giving you the things you quietly work for. I've been watching that pattern for years. It's getting predictable.

So until the next chapter catches up with you, be a little kinder to yourself.

Take care of the girl I love. She's done a lot this year. And she deserves someone firmly on her side, especially herself.

— always in your corner,
your person.
Chapter Five

You once asked me what you were.

You asked if you were the earth, the sun, the moon, or some other planet.

I said the earth, because the earth has life.

And that's you. You are the reason your people feel alive. You are the gravity that keeps us orbiting each other. You are the proof that something can hold so much, carry so much, and still grow things.

Two years later, I still stand by it.

Chapter Six

One wish, for the year ahead.

may this year bring you every success you've been working for.

every happiness you forget to ask for.

every brand on your wishlist — actually, make it two of each.

every place you've saved a pin on.

and if the universe has a slot open, maybe, just maybe, let us meet this year. it's been two years. at this point we're basically pen pals with better cameras.
